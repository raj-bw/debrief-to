Debrief.TO — who funds the newsrooms
====================================

One file: app/page.js

NOTE: this includes the Ontario town changes as well, since they are in the
same file. If you have not uploaded debrief-ontario-towns.zip yet, upload
that one FIRST (it has five other files), then this page.js on top.

WHAT IT ADDS

The About page said choosing the source list is "the one editorial decision
here" but said nothing about who funds those newsrooms. It now does, grouped
three ways:

  Reader-funded      memberships, donations, subscriptions; includes
                     registered non-profits and charities
  Publicly funded    government (in practice, the CBC)
  Commercially owned privately owned, ad- or subscriber-funded

Deliberately coarse. Anything finer could not be stated accurately for
fifteen organisations, and each one publishes its own funding on its own
site — which is where the links already go.

It also says plainly that these are funding models and not verdicts: a
commercially owned newsroom can do excellent reporting and a reader-funded
one can still have a point of view.

WORTH YOUR EYE BEFORE YOU PUBLISH

I am confident about CBC (public), Toronto Star (NordStar Capital) and the
Village Media outlets — TorontoToday and The Trillium — as commercial, and
about the non-profits: The Narwhal, The Walrus, IJF, The Green Line,
The Local.

Three I would sanity-check against their own About pages, because their
structures are less clear-cut and I would rather you confirm than take my
word on a public page:

  - Spacing Toronto   tagged commercial; it is a small independent magazine
  - National Observer tagged reader-funded; it is reader-supported but
                      for-profit
  - The Maple         tagged reader-funded; it merged with Passage in 2023,
                      so check how it now describes itself

Changing any of them is a one-word edit to the `funding` field in the
PUBLISHERS list at the top of app/page.js.
