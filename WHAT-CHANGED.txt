WHAT IT DOES

All 413 Ontario municipalities are now selectable, from Toronto down to
Cockburn Island. The list scrolls and searches. No API, no key, no billing,
and nothing about the reader is sent anywhere to look their town up — which
matters, because the About page promises their town never leaves their
browser and a third-party autocomplete would quietly break that.

The data is split in two, deliberately:

  WHICH PLACES EXIST  - public record, ships as an 8 KB file.
  WHO COVERS THEM     - editorial, ours, and the part that actually matters.

A reader's local news resolves in three steps:

  1. Their town has its own newsroom      -> tab is named after the town
                                             (Newmarket -> "Newmarket")
  2. Their region's newsroom covers them  -> tab is named after the region
                                             (Aurora -> "York Region")
  3. Nobody covers them yet               -> NO local tab at all

Case 3 is the honest one. A tab that is always empty is worse than no tab.
Those readers get a "Set your town" control instead, so they are never stuck,
and the picker tells them up front rather than after they choose.

The picker now names the actual newsroom against each town, so nobody picks
blind: "Aurora — Newmarket Today, York Region", "Kingston — No local
publisher yet". That list doubles as your map of where to add a publisher.

MIGRATION

Readers who already chose a town keep it. Slugs that changed are aliased
("sudbury" -> Greater Sudbury, "bradford" -> Bradford West Gwillimbury), and
Toronto's former boroughs (Scarborough, Etobicoke, North York) map to Toronto,
since they are not municipalities of their own.

SOURCE OF THE DATA

Every municipality and its census division came from the Wikipedia list of
municipalities in Ontario, read directly from the table rather than typed from
memory. 413 rows, 49 divisions, spot-checked against known answers.
