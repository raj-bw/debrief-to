1. THE TWO MIS-TAGS YOU SPOTTED

Both were real, and the second was a design flaw rather than a typo.

The Green Line piece about the City of Toronto's powers was tagged National
Politics. So was "Toronto Election 2026: Mayor's Debate" — a MUNICIPAL
election. Cause: any article a publisher tagged "Politics", "Government",
"Election" or "Policy" matched National Politics. Those words say what a
piece is about but nothing about its scale, and scale is what that tab
claims. Only unambiguously federal categories are accepted now; everything
else has to earn the tag through a headline naming the level of government.

The Breach's Doug Ford profile was tagged Environment because category
matching was checking substrings — so a category merely CONTAINING one of
our words counted. It now matches whole words only, and the broadest terms
("energy", "water", "nature") are gone.

Expect the National Politics tab to get thinner. That is the intended
trade: a thin accurate tab beats a fat wrong one. See the question I asked
you in chat about renaming it — that is the better fix.

Also: cards now show up to TWO topics instead of one. An article genuinely
can be both, and showing only the first made correct tagging look wrong.

2. PICKING A TOWN NOW SELECTS AND LOADS IT

Choose Guelph and the Guelph pill is active immediately with the feed
filtered to it. Previously you had to pick the town, then go and click its
chip. A town with no newsroom clears the filter instead, since there is no
tab to select.

3. TORONTO REMOVED FROM THE HOMETOWN LIST

Toronto already has its own place tab fed by the standing source list, so
offering it as a hometown put the same city in two chips side by side.
Anyone who had picked it now gets no local tab, which is correct — the
Toronto tab IS their local tab. Scarborough, Etobicoke, North York and East
York behave the same way.

4. WHO OWNS THEM, ON THE ABOUT PAGE

Under the funding section: "TorontoToday and The Trillium are Village
Media. Toronto Star is Torstar, owned by NordStar Capital. CBC Toronto is
CBC/Radio-Canada. The other 12 are independently owned, each by itself."

Generated from the data rather than typed, so it stays right as the list
grows. The point is not that a newsroom has an owner — they all do — but
that when several on the list share one, "four newsrooms covered this"
means less than it looks.

5. THE FEED VERIFIER — THIS IS THE BIG ONE

149 candidate publishers extracted from your research (74 town-level, 75
regional), covering every publication and URL in the document.

Visit, once deployed:

  https://debrief.to/api/verify-feeds

It tries eight common feed paths against each publisher and reports which
answer, how many items they carry and how stale they are. Ten at a time;
the response includes a "next" link for the following batch, so roughly 15
clicks covers all 149.

  /api/verify-feeds?limit=20            bigger batches
  /api/verify-feeds?scope=town          only the town-level ones
  /api/verify-feeds?offset=40           jump in partway

It is read-only and changes nothing. Send me the output and I will turn the
winners into the actual source list.

I could not run it from here — publishers block my sandbox, and even DNS
fails. That is exactly why it has to run on Vercel.

Expect a meaningful share to fail. WordPress and Village Media sites nearly
always have feeds; Metroland portals and broadcaster sites often do not.
